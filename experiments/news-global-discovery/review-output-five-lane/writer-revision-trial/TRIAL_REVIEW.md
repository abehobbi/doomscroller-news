### Astronomers identify origin of 10.6 billion-year-old fast radio burst

Astronomers have pinpointed the host galaxy of the most distant fast radio burst (FRB) identified to date. The signal, designated FRB 20240304B, originated from a small galaxy approximately 10.6 billion years ago, a time when the universe was only one-fifth of its current age. This discovery, published in the journal Science on October 8, 2026, provides evidence that these energetic events were occurring as early as 3 billion years after the Big Bang, during a period of intense star and galaxy formation.

Fast radio bursts are millisecond-long flashes of radio emission from the distant universe. First discovered in 2007, their exact origin remains an enigma because most are observed only once. Researchers have theories regarding the objects that produce them, but they lack conclusive proof of what generates the bursts. Identifying the host galaxy of FRB 20240304B allows scientists to study the environment in which these bursts occur, which may offer clues to their nature.

The burst was detected on March 4, 2024, by the MeerKAT radio telescope in South Africa using MeerTRAP, a system designed to capture fast-changing transient radio signals in real time. Researchers used a phenomenon called dispersion to calculate the distance; this occurs when different frequencies of a radio burst arrive at slightly different times because the signal traveled through electrically charged material in space. This calculation confirmed the signal originated from a galaxy 10.6 billion years ago.

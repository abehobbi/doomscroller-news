### China proposes first nationwide labor rules for gig economy workers

China’s Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security released a 54-article draft rule on October 8, 2026, that would establish the country’s first ministerial regulation specifically governing the rights of workers in new forms of employment. The proposed measures target workers such as food-delivery riders, couriers, ride-hailing drivers, and truck drivers who often fall outside conventional labor-law definitions due to flexible and complex employment arrangements. If adopted, the rules would bring workers who are subject to labor management by enterprises—but whose arrangements do not fully meet the criteria for establishing a formal labor relationship—under the protection of the labor legal system through departmental rules.

The draft proposal includes several key protections and regulatory requirements:  • Minimum-pay safeguards and protections for basic labor rights and interests.  • Limits and regulations on algorithmic decision-making and labor rules.  • Requirements for transparent rules and breaks to protect rest rights.  • Labor protections during severe weather, addressing the outdoor nature of much gig work.  • Provisions to facilitate the resolution of labor disputes.  • Clarification of regulatory responsibilities for government departments and legal liabilities for violations.  Shen Jianfeng, a law professor at the Central University of Finance and Economics, noted that while parts of the draft adapt traditional labor standards to platform conditions, the provisions regarding data protection and algorithm rules represent institutional innovations.

The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security is seeking public comment on the draft through Nov. 8, 2026. Members of the public can submit feedback via the ministry’s website, by mail, or via email.

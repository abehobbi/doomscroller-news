Trade envoys from the European Union and China agreed on October 9, 2026, to a broad initial deal intended to address trade imbalances and escalating tensions between the two economies. European Commissioner for Trade Maros Sefcovic stated the agreement could reduce Chinese imports of electric and plug-in hybrid vehicles to the EU by up to 50% over the next four years, a move he said would prevent several million car exports from China to the bloc. This reduction follows concerns that surging shipments of low-priced Chinese vehicles, including battery electric vehicles and hybrids—which combine an internal combustion engine with a small battery—could threaten European manufacturers and jobs. Sefcovic noted this is the first time China has agreed to moderate exports without the EU first undergoing the standard investigations required to implement trade safeguards under World Trade Organization rules.

The preliminary deal includes measures to stabilize rare earth supply chains. Sefcovic said both sides reached understandings to further facilitate China’s export licensing for rare earths and permanent magnets. Additionally, the agreement aims to improve access to the Chinese market for various European products through lower most-favored-nation tariffs. These products, which include car parts, olive oil, and footwear, represent nearly 4 billion euros ($4.5 billion) in current export value and could benefit almost every EU nation. While the deal offers concessions for European exporters, China’s Commerce Minister Wang Wentao expressed concerns regarding recent restrictive measures taken by the EU, according to a post by China’s Commerce Ministry. The agreement still requires approval from leaders across the 27-nation EU.

The negotiations follow a period of widening trade deficits. EU statistics show the trade deficit with China grew to 103.3 billion euros ($116 billion) in the second quarter, as imports rose to 153.6 billion euros ($172 billion) while exports to China reached 50.3 billion euros ($56 billion). The two sides had been seeking to resolve factors behind China’s growing trade surplus, which reached 360 billion euros ($403 billion). Regarding foreign investment, the German economic ministry stated that while Germany welcomes investment, certain acquisitions could endanger national security by deepening dependencies and jeopardizing the resilience of supply chains in Germany and the EU.

Research into the life of Fred Vanke, a 103-year-old living in Breaston, Derbyshire, has led to the addition of 27 Polish codebreakers and signals intelligence (sigint) specialists to the Bletchley Park Roll of Honour. This expansion of the documented Polish contribution to Britain's wartime codebreaking effort is one of the most significant in recent years, helping historians understand the intelligence partnership between Britain and the Polish government-in-exile. Fred Vanke, who was sworn to secrecy regarding his wartime activities, revealed his history after research into his life story. Vanke noted that he never told anyone about his specific role, including his wife, Patricia, who died in 1996 without knowing the nature of his work.

Vanke's military service involved several stages. After the invasion of France, his intended destination of Syria was lost, leading him to British-controlled Palestine. There, he joined the Carpathian Rifle Brigade alongside his father. Following officer training in Tel Aviv, he was sent to Glasgow to join the Cichociemni, a unit he described as meaning 'dark and quiet' in Polish. While Vanke served in these capacities, he expressed pride in the work of Zygalski, Rejewski, and Różycki, whom he identified as the 'three real codebreakers.' Following the war, Vanke moved to Nottingham, where he started a photography firm with a fellow Polish ex-paratrooper.

Some of Thomas's murals address environmental damage and colonial violence, specifically the ongoing health effects that abandoned uranium mines have on Diné families. India relies on imports to meet more than 80% of its domestic silver demand, and its purchases represent over 20 percent of the global trade in refined silver. Recent data shows silver imports plunged by 87 percent to $75.6 million in a single month, down from $566.2 million the previous year, marking the country's lowest import level in over three years.

## 19. Astronomers pinpoint origin of 10 billion-year-old fast radio burst

Lane: Discovery

Image: [theconversation.com](https://images.theconversation.com/files/764409/original/file-20261007-50-3g8dq3.png?ixlib=rb-4.1.1&rect=850%2C1288%2C3503%2C1751&q=45&auto=format&w=1356&h=668&fit=crop)

Astronomers have identified the host galaxy of the most distant fast radio burst (FRB) recorded to date. The signal, designated FRB 20240304B, originated in a small galaxy approximately 10.6 billion years ago, when the universe was roughly one-fifth of its current age. This discovery, published in the journal Science on October 8, 2026, provides new evidence regarding the timing of these energetic cosmic events, showing they were being produced as early as 3 billion years after the Big Bang during a period of intense star and galaxy formation. Fast radio bursts are millisecond-long flashes of radio emission from the distant universe. First discovered in 2007, their exact origins remain uncertain because most are seen only once and never again.

The detection began on March 4, 2024, when the MeerTRAP system—designed to catch fast-changing transient radio signals in real time—detected the burst using the MeerKAT radio telescope in South Africa. To determine the distance, researchers analyzed the signal's dispersion, an effect where different frequencies of the radio burst arrive at slightly different times because the signal traveled through electrically charged material in space. While MeerKAT data localized the burst and suggested it was extremely distant, astronomers required NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope to study the host galaxy and accurately measure the distance. The identified host galaxy is a young, small galaxy with a low metal content and a mass only about 10 million times that of the Sun, which is a small fraction of the Milky Way's mass. This differs from many larger galaxies where most other fast radio bursts have been found. Manisha Caleb of the University of Sydney, the lead author of the study, noted that while there are theories regarding what generates these bursts, conclusive proof of their origin is still lacking.

Lane: world-major

Ethiopia and Eritrea have exchanged accusations of aggression at the United Nations, raising fears of a new conflict in the Horn of Africa as fighting intensifies in Ethiopia’s northern Tigray region. The diplomatic spat follows allegations that Eritrean troops have crossed the border into Ethiopia to support rebels fighting the Ethiopian government. On October 9, 2026, Ethiopia told the UN Security Council that Eritrean troops had advanced as far as 60km (37 miles) into Tigray, accusing its neighbor of violating its sovereignty. Ethiopian Foreign Minister Gedion Timothewos described the alleged incursion in a letter to the UN as a “blatant act of aggression” and invoked Article 51 of the UN Charter, which recognizes a country’s right to self-defense, though he did not specify what measures Ethiopia was taking. Eritrea rejected these accusations, calling them a pretext for an offensive by Addis Ababa. In a letter to the UN Security Council on October 10, 2026, Eritrean Foreign Minister Osman Saleh urged the body to condemn Ethiopia’s “deplorable declaration of war” and take “deterrent measures,” warning that an Ethiopian offensive would cause “huge devastation to the peoples of both countries and destabilise the whole region.”

The escalation follows a period of deteriorating relations between the two neighbors, who were allies during the 2020-2022 Tigray war but have since been at loggerheads over the 2022 peace agreement and Ethiopia’s demands for access to the Red Sea. On October 5, 2026, when Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed was sworn in for another five years, he stated that access to the sea was a priority, fueling speculation that he intends to take Eritrea's strategic port of Assab by force. While the Ethiopian government has ruled out the use of force to pursue sea access, Eritrea has viewed the claim as an affront. Current fighting in Tigray involves the Ethiopian government against a coalition of seven rebel groups, spearheaded by the Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF). This coalition includes groups such as the Amhara Fano National Movement (AFNM), the Afar Revolutionary Democratic Unity Front (ARDUF), and the Oromo Liberation Army. Eritrea has used proxy politics—fostering armed groups inside Ethiopia to offset its relative limitations—to influence the region. Various sources have confirmed the presence of Eritrean armed forces in Ethiopia, with an AFP report quoting a foreign security source estimating that between 5,000 and 8,000 Eritrean soldiers were present in Tigray.

## 4. Coalition condemns US decision to impose sanctions on the International Criminal Court

Lane: world-major

The Coalition for the International Criminal Court (CICC), representing more than 120 organizations from over 60 countries, has issued a joint statement condemning the United States' decision to impose sanctions on the International Criminal Court (ICC) as an institution. On 9 October 2026, the US announced these sanctions under Executive Order 14203. The CICC, which includes legal experts, human rights defenders, and members of the European Parliament, described the measure as an unprecedented attack against the rule of law and the independence of international justice. The coalition stated that the move undermines the rights of millions of victims and survivors of mass atrocities worldwide and called for coordinated action from States Parties to protect the court's ability to function.

The sanctions, announced by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, target the ICC itself. Rubio stated the US would ban transactions with the court to cut off resources and prevent it from operating against the US, asserting that the American people are not subject to the jurisdiction of the ICC. The Trump administration has stated its aim is to dismantle the ICC “brick by brick,” alleging the court has gone beyond its remit by investigating crimes allegedly committed by US nationals or their allies, specifically Israeli nationals, on the territory of State Parties. Under the Rome Statute, the treaty establishing the court, the ICC may exercise jurisdiction over crimes committed on the territory of a State Party regardless of the nationality of the alleged perpetrator. The US and Israel are not parties to the Rome Statute.

Executive Order 14203 prohibits US persons from providing material support or services, such as financial services, to designated entities like the ICC. It also exposes non-US persons to the risk of secondary sanctions for engaging in prohibited conduct. This institutional sanction follows a campaign by the Trump administration that began in February 2025, which targeted the former ICC Prosecutor Karim Khan, both Deputy Prosecutors, the ICC President, eight other judges, a staff member, a judicial candidate, a UN Special Rapporteur, and three Palestinian human rights organisations. The CICC maintains these measures aim to undermine the Rome Statute system as a whole to prevent the investigation and prosecution of international crimes.

While the full scale of the impact is unclear, individual chain reports suggest approximately 400 stores were affected out of roughly 15,000 in France. The attack may not be confined to France; Felix Aime, a senior cyber threat intelligence analyst at Sekoia Threat Detection and Research, said Spain, Portugal, Poland, Germany, and Belgium had also been affected. ## 11. NATO commander unveils “Theory of Victory” to counter potential Russian attacks

Lane: world-major

NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, U.S. General Alexus Grynkewich, outlined a new military strategy called the “Theory of Victory” at the Warsaw Security Forum on October 12, 2026. The three-part blueprint is designed to ensure that any attack on a NATO member proves “utterly futile” by blocking, blunting, and breaking enemy forces. Grynkewich stated that while the alliance exists to preserve peace and he does not assess that conflict is “imminent or inevitable,” the 32-member alliance is “ready to fight tonight” if attacked. The strategy aims to deter adversaries, specifically amid concerns that Russia could be capable of attacking a NATO member by the end of the decade and following a spate of sabotage, arson, and drone incursions across Europe.

The “Theory of Victory” functions through three distinct stages of defense. First, NATO would use land-based troops, supported by air, naval, cyber, and space capabilities, to “block” an initial assault and prevent the seizure of alliance territory. Second, the alliance would “blunt” the attack by using sensors and autonomous systems to overwhelm enemy formations and prevent further advances. Finally, if the aggression continues, NATO would “break” the enemy's warfighting system. This final stage involves using highly effective precision munitions and unmanned systems to destroy not only the attacking forces but also the systems that support, sustain, and control them. Grynkewich noted that the plan draws on lessons from modern warfare, particularly fighting in Ukraine, and incorporates advanced technologies like artificial intelligence and counter-drone systems.

The unveiling of this military blueprint comes during a period of internal and external pressure for the alliance. Grynkewich's intervention follows a year marked by infighting over the U.S.-Israeli war in Iran and threats from Donald Trump to annex Greenland. Additionally, the alliance faces concerns regarding U.S. commitment, as President Trump has reportedly agreed to a deal to buy diesel from Russia and has suggested pulling American troops from Europe. Addressing these tensions, Grynkewich emphasized that while troop deployment is a political decision, “no one should question the U.S. commitment to the alliance.” Lithuanian Defense Minister Robertas Kaunas supported the strategy, stating it sends a strong message to adversaries that the alliance will defend itself.

## 30. Organic molecules found in planet-forming discs around distant stars

## 31. Astronomers attribute 10-minute X-ray burst to colliding neutron stars

Lane: discovery

Astronomers have attributed a mysterious X-ray burst, known as EP250704a, to the collision of two neutron stars. The event began with a half-second gamma-ray burst followed by approximately 10 minutes of X-ray emissions, a duration about 1,200 times longer than the initial gamma-ray spike. This long-lasting X-ray emission caught the attention of researchers, including Eleonora Troja, a member of the Einstein Probe European collaboration, who noted the unusual nature of the data upon seeing it. While neutron-star mergers are typically associated with gamma-ray flashes that fade within two seconds, new research suggests these collisions can also produce X-ray blasts lasting several minutes.

To explain the extended duration of the X-ray burst, the research team suggests the involvement of a magnetar. Magnetars are rapidly spinning neutron stars characterized by the strongest magnetic fields in the universe. The researchers propose that if the remnant of the collision is a magnetar, it could continue bursting for a longer period by dumping its magnetic power into its surroundings, making the explosion brighter and more long-lasting. This process may occur because a collapsing stellar core can spin a resulting neutron star at more than 700 rotations per second and squeeze magnetic field lines together, strengthening the field. The team's interpretation of the event as a merger rests on ruling out a supernova, though the provided data from the VLT or VLA does not describe any positive signs of a merger, such as a kilonova light.

The discovery provides a test case for hundreds of X-ray flashes logged by the Einstein Probe since its 2024 launch, some of which remain unexplained. Neutron-star mergers are significant cosmic events credited with forging elements such as gold, silver, and platinum. The study of EP250704a helps astronomers understand the diverse ways these high-energy collisions can manifest in the universe.

## 32. New Jurassic crocodile relative had slender teeth for eating small aquatic prey

Lane: discovery

Scientists have discovered a new species of ancient crocodile relative that lived approximately 170 million years ago. This unique dental structure suggests the creature fed on small aquatic prey, such as tiny fish and crustaceans like fairy shrimp, rather than hunting large animals. After examining the fossils under a microscope and using a scalpel to uncover more of the structures, the researchers realized they were incredibly thin teeth. Published in the Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology, the research details how microscopic examination of the teeth's internal structure revealed specialized ridges and close spacing, supporting the conclusion that the species had a highly unusual diet.

## 33. Increased brain entropy during psilocybin trips tracks later gains in well-being

Lane: discovery

Participants who experienced more irregular brain activity during a high dose of psilocybin tended to show the largest gains in well-being one month later, according to a study published in Nature Communications. The research tracked healthy volunteers who had never previously taken a psychedelic to see if the acute disruptions caused by the drug leave enduring marks on the brain. In addition to well-being, the study found improvements in psychological insight and cognitive flexibility—the ability to adapt thinking and switch between different rules or concepts—one month after the dose. While researchers detected possible changes in the brain's white matter, lasting changes in brain function were largely absent.

Psilocybin is the active psychedelic compound found in “magic mushrooms” that works primarily by stimulating a specific type of serotonin receptor in the brain. Some scientists have proposed that this chemical action scrambles normal brain activity into a more flexible, highly disordered state. This concept is central to the “entropic brain” hypothesis, introduced in a 2014 theoretical paper by Robin Carhart-Harris, the senior author of the new study. The hypothesis suggests that psychedelics expand conscious experience by making brain signals more unpredictable, or increasing brain entropy.
